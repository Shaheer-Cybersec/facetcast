# Night Shift

*A photo essay on one street between 2 AM and 4 AM.*

This is my BFA thesis project (Photography, final year). For six weeks I walked the same
400 metres of Kettle Lane every night between 2 AM and 4 AM and photographed what the
street does when nobody is watching it.

I wanted to find out one thing: does a street have a personality when its people are gone?

## What's in here

- `chapters/` - the written part of the thesis, one file per chapter
- `frames/index.csv` - the 48 final frames, with date, time, lens and a one-line note
- `notes/` - nothing yet, my field notes are still on paper

## How I worked

- One camera, one 35mm lens, no flash, no tripod. If the street was too dark, the frame was too dark.
- Same route every night, starting at the bakery and ending at the bus depot.
- I shot film for the first two weeks and switched to digital in week three, because sodium
  street lamps turned every roll orange and I could not afford to keep fixing it in the darkroom.
- 2,100 exposures in total. 48 made the final edit.

## What I found

Honestly? The street is louder at 3 AM than at noon - just with different noises. The
bakery starts at 3:10. The bins go out at 3:40. A fox crosses near number 27 most nights.

The written part argues that "empty" streets are never empty, they are just working for
somebody else.
